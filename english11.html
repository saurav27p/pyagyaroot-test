Teacher's Insight

What this unit is really testing

Unit 11 pairs two texts that make opposite arguments about human nature. The Two Little Girls shows children who forget a quarrel within minutes and go back to playing. The adults around them hold on to the same quarrel until it nearly becomes a fight. Tolstoy's point is that moral sense is not learned. It is lost. The children already have it. The adults have forgotten it.

World Record is a satire. It shows adults chasing pointless records on live television. A man stands on one leg in hot soup for seven hours. Another leans on a brush. A third wanders on set without trousers. The host treats all of it as entertainment. The play asks a simple question through comedy. What counts as achievement when the achievement itself is meaningless.

Exam weight of this unit. Negative sentences and subject-verb agreement appear in the grammar section. The moral story and the condolence message belong to the writing section. Reading comprehension from this unit can appear in Reading 1 or Reading 2 as a textbook extract.

Summary of Reading I: The Two Little Girls

The story is about two little girls named Malasha and Akoulya. They met in a lane between two homesteads where dirty water had formed a large puddle. Their mothers had dressed them both in new frocks. They had just come from church and first showed each other their finery. Soon the fancy took them to splash about in the water.

Akoulya stopped Malasha from stepping into the puddle with her shoes on. They both took off their shoes and stockings and began walking through the puddle. When they got near each other Malasha plumped down her foot and the water splashed right onto Akoulya's frock. Akoulya got angry and ran after Malasha to strike her. Malasha scrambled out of the puddle and prepared to run home.

Just then Akoulya's mother happened to be passing. She saw her daughter's dirty frock and asked what had happened. Akoulya said Malasha did it on purpose. Akoulya's mother seized Malasha and struck her on the back of her neck. Malasha began to howl. Her mother came out and began scolding her neighbour. One word led to another and they had an angry quarrel. The men came out and a crowd collected in the street. The affair had very nearly come to blows.

Akoulya's old grandmother stepped in and tried to calm them but they would not listen. While the women were abusing each other Akoulya had wiped the mud off her frock and gone back to the puddle. She took a stone and began scraping away the earth to make a channel. Malasha joined her and with a chip of wood helped her dig the channel. The water ran streaming into the street towards the very place where the old woman was trying to pacify the men. The girls followed it one running each side of the little stream.

The old woman said to the men that they should be ashamed of themselves. The little girls had forgotten all about their quarrel and were playing happily together. The men looked at the little girls and were ashamed. They laughed at themselves and went back each to his own home. The story teaches that we should forgive and forget like little children.

Student Notes

What to remember from this unit

Reading I in one sentence. Two girls quarrel over splashed water, their mothers turn it into a street fight, and the girls themselves forget the quarrel and go back to playing.

Memory device for Reading I. Two girls, two mothers, one puddle. The girls forget. The mothers do not. The men fight. The grandmother speaks sense. The girls end the fight without knowing they ended it.

Memory device for Reading II. Four people on stage. Albert stands on one leg in hot soup. Daisy pours more soup every half hour. Mabel leans on a brush. A stranger looks for his trousers. Mabel pushes Albert at the ten second mark. He breaks his leg instead of the record.

Common Mistakes

Errors seen in real SEE scripts

  • Confusing finery with fancy. Finery means fine clothes and jewellery. Fancy means a sudden desire or whim. Question A tests both. Students mix them up because they sound similar.
  • Choosing the wrong alternative in Question B. The question asks why the girls were dressed in new frocks. Most students pick the first option because it sounds nice. The correct answer is the one the passage supports: they had just come from church.
  • Missing the lesson. The lesson is not that children should obey adults. It is that adults should learn from children. The girls forgive in minutes. The adults do not. The SEE answer must reflect this direction.
  • Using much in negative transformations of a few. "He ate a few mangoes" becomes "He did not eat many mangoes" not "He did not eat a few mangoes." The quantity word changes with the negation.
  • Changing all of to none of incorrectly. "All of them can help her" becomes "None of them can help her" not "All of them cannot help her." English does not allow two negatives in one clause.
  • Mismatching subject and verb in the letter. "Here is my resume" not "Here are my resume." "Everyone appreciates" not "Everyone appreciate." The letter exercise in this unit tests exactly these cases.
  • Writing the condolence as a personal letter. A condolence message is not written to the deceased. It is written about the deceased, addressed to the family. The tone is formal and restrained.

Grammar Revision

Negative Sentences

The idea

A negative sentence says that something is not true or does not happen. English forms negatives in different ways depending on the verb. Understanding which rule applies is the whole exercise in Grammar I.

The rules

Verb typeRuleExample
be (is, am, are, was, were)Add not after the verbShe is happy. becomes She is not happy.
can, must, should, will (modals)Add not after the modalHe can help. becomes He cannot help.
Simple present, other verbsAdd do not or does not before the verb. Drop s from the main verbShe works. becomes She does not work.
Simple past, other verbsAdd did not before the verb. Change the verb back to base formHe ate. becomes He did not eat.
Present perfectAdd not after has or haveShe has come. becomes She has not come.
a few, a littleChange to many, much in the negativeHe ate a few mangoes. becomes He did not eat many mangoes.
allChange to noneAll can help. becomes None can help.
sometimes, always, oftenReplace the word with its negative counterpartHe is sometimes late. becomes He is never late.
ever, anything, someoneChange to never, nothing, no oneShe has something. becomes She has nothing.

How to solve the exercise

Look at the verb first. Then pick the rule. Do not change the tense. Do not add or remove information. The only change is the negation.

Common traps

  • Do not write "All of them cannot help her." English does not allow two negatives in one clause. Use "None of them can help her."
  • Do not write "He did not ate" or "She does not works." The main verb goes back to base form.
  • Do not add not with do or does when the verb is be. "She is not happy" is correct. "She does not is happy" is wrong.

Writing I

You might have read or heard some moral stories. Write your favourite one in your own words.

Answer:

The Hare and the Tortoise

Once upon a time a hare and a tortoise lived in a forest. The hare was very proud of his speed. He always mocked the tortoise for being slow. One day the tortoise challenged the hare to a race. The hare laughed but accepted the challenge.

The race began. The hare ran very fast and soon left the tortoise far behind. He thought he had plenty of time to rest. He lay down under a tree and fell asleep. The tortoise kept walking slowly but steadily. He did not stop anywhere.

When the hare woke up he saw that the tortoise was near the finish line. The hare ran as fast as he could but it was too late. The tortoise had already won the race.

Moral: Slow and steady wins the race.

Summary of Reading II: World Record

The text is a comedy show set in a big TV studio. The host Michael Moonshine presents a program that gives people the chance to break world records. Two people are trying to break world records.

Albert Hargreaves is standing on one leg in a bucket of hot soup. He has been standing there for almost seven hours. The world record is seven hours and three minutes. His wife Daisy pours sizzling soup into the bucket every half hour to keep it hot. Albert is very close to breaking the record.

Mabel Phillips is another contender. She is leaning on a brush. She is a very charming young lady. While Albert is about to break his record a man appears without trousers. He is not trying to break a record. He is just looking for his trousers that went missing while he was having a bath.

When Albert has only ten seconds left Mabel pushes him. Albert falls over and breaks his leg instead of breaking the world record.

E. How do you feel if somebody interrupts you on the verge of winning point? Do you think it is ethical?

Answer:
If somebody interrupts me on the verge of winning I would feel very frustrated and disappointed. All my hard work and practice would be wasted because of someone else's unfair action. It is not ethical to interrupt someone when they are about to achieve their goal. Everyone should have a fair chance to succeed. Mabel's action in the play was wrong because she pushed Albert and made him fall. She should have let him complete his attempt. Healthy competition means doing your best without harming others.
Grammar Revision

Subject-Verb Agreement

The idea

The subject and the verb in a sentence must match in number. Singular subject takes singular verb. Plural subject takes plural verb. English has many cases where this is easy to get wrong.

The rules

CaseRuleExample
a number ofTakes plural verbA number of soldiers were injured.
the number ofTakes singular verbThe number of soldiers is small.
a pack of, a group ofTakes singular verbA pack of lions was approaching.
pants, scissors, trousersTakes plural verbHis pants were torn.
the + adjective (the rich, the truthful)Takes plural verbThe truthful are always trustworthy.
to + verb as subjectTakes singular verbTo cry is never the solution.
here, thereVerb agrees with the noun after the verbHere lies the tomb. Here are the books.
either or, neither norVerb agrees with the nearer subjectEither she or her friends are responsible.
each, every, everyoneTakes singular verbEach of the dancers twirls brilliantly.
noneTakes singular or plural depending on meaningNone is under the sun.
all that, everythingTakes singular verbAll that glitters is not gold.
I along with, together withVerb agrees with the first subjectI along with my colleagues am efficient.

How to solve the exercise

Find the real subject first. Ignore phrases between the subject and the verb. Then check whether the subject is singular or plural. Then match the verb.

Common traps

  • Do not match the verb with the noun right before it. Match with the real subject. "A pack of lions" is singular because a pack is the subject, not lions.
  • Do not mix singular and plural in "either or" or "neither nor" sentences. The verb follows the nearer subject.
  • Do not write "Here is my books." Use "Here are my books" because books is plural.
